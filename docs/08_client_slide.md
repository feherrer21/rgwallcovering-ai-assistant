# For Ronald — one page

## The problem, in your terms

Someone lands on rgwallcovering.com at 9pm with a hallway they want papered.
They want to know three things: do you cover their town, roughly what it
costs, and how to start. **The site answers none of them** — and the Services
page currently serves template text about wind turbines, live, today.

So they leave. You never learn they were there, because nothing on the site
captures anything.

## What this does

An assistant on your site that answers from **what you have already
published**, and when there is a real project behind the questions, gets the
details and emails you a summary before you ever pick up the phone.

> *"How much would something like that run us?"*
>
> **"I can't put a number on it — every project is priced after the space is
> assessed, since prep work, wall condition and material choice move the cost
> far more than square footage does. Roughly how big is the reception area,
> and do you have a look in mind already?"**

It will not invent your prices. But it does give the ones you have told it:

> *"Do you charge for the estimate, or is it free?"*
>
> **"There's a $300 assessment fee — it's never free, but it comes off the
> cost of the installation if you go ahead. That covers about an hour on site
> taking measurements; for projects needing a longer assessment the fee is
> $500, and the team confirms which one applies to yours."**

Four turns later, this is in your inbox — subject line readable from a locked
phone, reply button that goes straight to her:

```
Subject: New enquiry — Elena Vasquez (commercial)

Elena runs a small dental practice in Providence and the reception walls are
looking tired. There is old vinyl wallpaper on roughly 30 linear feet of wall
that would need to come off first, so wall condition and prep will be the main
unknowns. She has no material or style picked out and was glad to hear design
help is available. She asked about cost and I told her pricing comes after an
assessment; she wants the work done before the new year.

Name: Elena Vasquez · Email: elena.vasquez@example.com
Project type: commercial · Location: Providence, RI
Space: Dental practice reception, roughly 30 linear feet
Needs design help: yes · Timing: before the new year
```

You know what she wants, how far along she is, and **what she was already
promised** — before the call starts.

<sup>Both quotes are real output, not mock-ups. Elena is a synthetic persona —
no real customer's details appear anywhere in this project. The captured run
predates 2026-08-14 and contained one further sentence, telling her the visit
would not be charged because she is in Providence; that fact was wrong and the
sentence is removed here rather than silently corrected. Why it was wrong is
the next section.</sup>

## The rule it runs on

**It never states a price, a timeline, a warranty term or a coverage area that
you have not published or confirmed.** Not even a hedged one, not even a
plausible one — because a made-up figure is not a wrong answer, it is a
commitment your customer will hold you to.

Tested against 30 questions, eight of them written specifically to pull a
number out of it. **Zero invented figures.** When it doesn't know, it says the
team confirms that directly and offers to pass the details along — which is
also how a good employee answers.

## What it still gets wrong — and the one thing only you can catch

**It repeated something you told me that turned out not to be true, and
nothing in the system noticed.**

On 12 August you said the assessment visit was free nearby and charged only
for longer trips. That went in as fact, and for two days the assistant told
people in Providence their visit was free. On 14 August you corrected it —
$300, the same for everyone. Fixed the same day.

This is worth a minute of your attention, because it is the limit of what the
build can do for you. Everything in it is designed to stop the assistant
inventing things you never said. It has no way to tell that something you
*did* say is wrong. **So the assistant is exactly as accurate as the last
thing you told it**, and the only check that works is you reading back what it
says about your business. Fifteen minutes, once.

Two smaller ones, both measured, neither hidden:

- Asked *"where are you based?"* it answers correctly but from a fixed note
  rather than your site, because the search ranks a blog post above your own
  contact page.
- It correctly tells people you don't do flooring, but that "no" is my
  inference from what you told me you *do* — you have never confirmed the
  list is complete. Which is why it is question 1 below.

## What it needs from you

You have already answered five of these, and it shows: the visit, the quote
turnaround and customer-supplied paper all went from *"the team will confirm"*
to a real answer the same afternoon, with no engineering work at all. What is
left:

1. **Is your service list complete?** Wallcovering, removal, murals, painting,
   design — is that everything, and what do people ask for that you always
   turn down? The second half is the valuable half: those are the enquiries
   worth deflecting before they cost you a call.
2. **Your contract.** It settles two things you were unsure of — the warranty
   term (you said six months, then thought the law might say a year) and the
   clause about paper the customer bought themselves. Until then the assistant
   says a warranty exists but won't put a length on it.
3. **The Services page** — it is broken and live, serving text about wind
   turbines. Fix it and the assistant gets better for free, because it reads
   that page.
4. **Where leads should land.** Assumed `info@rgwallcovering.com`; one line to
   change.

## What it costs to run

A few cents per conversation, and it runs on your existing website — no new
system to log into, no dashboard to remember, no monthly seat you forget you
are paying for. **The email is the whole product.**

This describes what generally drives the duration of wallcovering work in the
trade. It is not a statement of any particular company's timelines.

## The factors that move the number

**Area and layout.** A single flat accent wall is a fundamentally different
job from a stairwell, a room with many windows and doors, or a space with
curved or sloped surfaces. Cuts around obstacles take far longer per square
foot than open wall does, so two rooms of identical size can differ
substantially.

**Condition of the wall underneath.** This is the factor that most often
surprises people. A smooth, sound, previously prepared wall can be covered
directly. A wall with old covering still on it, damage, damp, heavy texture or
unsuitable paint needs work before anything goes up, and that preparation can
take longer than the installation itself.

**The pattern.** A plain or randomly textured material is quick. A pattern
that must be matched across every seam is slower, and the larger the pattern
repeat, the slower it goes — and the more material is consumed as offcuts. A
bespoke mural, printed as numbered panels for one specific wall, requires
precise sequencing.

**The material.** Different substrates behave differently. Some are hung with
paste applied to the wall, others to the material itself; some are heavy and
need two people; delicate finishes such as grasscloth or silk are unforgiving
of handling marks and slow to hang well.

**Access and scheduling.** Occupied homes need furniture moved and protected.
Commercial spaces frequently restrict work to evenings, weekends or phased
areas so the business can keep operating, which stretches a job across more
calendar days than it takes in working hours.

## The distinction worth drawing

There are two different questions people usually mean by "how long":

- **How long until I get a quote** — depends on how quickly the space can be
  seen and measured.
- **How long will the work take** — depends on everything above.

Both are specific to the project and to the company doing it.

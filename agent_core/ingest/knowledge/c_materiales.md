General background on the main families of wallcovering and what each is
suited to. Which of these any particular company offers is a separate
question.

## Non-woven

The most common modern substrate. A blend of natural and synthetic fibres that
is dimensionally stable, meaning it does not expand when it gets wet and does
not shrink as it dries. Usually hung by pasting the wall rather than the
material, which is faster and cleaner, and it is normally strippable in one
piece later. A sensible default for most residential rooms.

## Vinyl

A printed backing with a vinyl surface. Wipeable, hard-wearing, tolerant of
moisture. Contract-grade vinyls are the standard choice for commercial and
high-traffic settings, and are manufactured to defined durability and fire
performance classes. Heavier and less breathable than paper, which matters on
walls prone to damp.

## Traditional paper

Printed paper, sometimes with a decorative coating. Often the best reproduction
of historical and hand-blocked designs, and favoured in period settings. Less
tolerant of moisture and handling than the alternatives, and expands when
pasted, so it demands more skill to hang well.

## Natural fibre — grasscloth, sisal, silk

Woven natural material on a paper backing. Texture and depth that printed
finishes cannot reproduce. Two properties matter and are often not explained
to clients beforehand: seams are visible by nature, since a natural fibre
cannot be pattern-matched, and colour varies between rolls. Both are inherent
to the material rather than defects. Not suited to humid rooms or heavy
contact.

## Murals and bespoke printing

An image printed to the dimensions of one specific wall and supplied as
numbered panels. Anything can be produced — a photograph, artwork, a scaled
pattern. Because it is made for one wall it must be measured precisely before
printing, and a change afterwards means reprinting rather than reordering.

## Textured and specialty finishes

Materials with relief, metallic, mica or fabric surfaces, and decorative paint
finishes applied on site. Effect depends heavily on lighting, which is why
these in particular are worth seeing as a sample in the actual room.

## What usually decides the choice

Where the wall is and what happens to it — moisture, contact, cleaning; how the
space is used and by whom; any regulatory requirement in a commercial setting;
the condition of the wall underneath, since some materials are more forgiving
of an imperfect surface than others; and budget, which ranges more widely
across these families than most people expect.

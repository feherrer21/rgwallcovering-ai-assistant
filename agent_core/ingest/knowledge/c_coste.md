This explains why wallcovering is quoted per project rather than sold at a
list price. It contains no prices, and none can be inferred from it.

## Why there is no price list

Two projects covering the same square footage can differ several-fold in cost,
because the price is the sum of things that vary independently:

**The material.** This is usually the widest-ranging variable. Machine-printed
papers, commercial-grade vinyls, natural fibres such as grasscloth, hand-
finished and bespoke printed murals occupy very different price brackets. The
same room can be finished at a fraction of the cost or a multiple of it purely
by the material chosen, before anyone picks up a tool.

**How much material the pattern consumes.** Cost is not driven by wall area
alone but by how much material must be bought to cover it. A large pattern
repeat that has to align across seams generates offcuts that cannot be used
elsewhere, so the quantity purchased can exceed the area covered
considerably.

**Labour, which is driven by complexity rather than size.** Cuts around
windows, doors, switches, radiators and mouldings; ceiling height and whether
access equipment is needed; stairwells; curved or sloped surfaces. An open
wall is quick per square foot; a complicated one is not.

**Preparation, the item most often forgotten.** Removing existing covering,
repairing damage, skimming heavy texture, sealing or priming. This is real
work with real cost and it is invisible until someone looks at the actual
wall.

**Access and timing.** Occupied spaces need protection and furniture moved.
Commercial work often has to happen outside business hours or in phases,
which costs more than the same work done in one uninterrupted run.

## What this means for someone asking

A number given without seeing the space is a guess, and a guess that turns out
low is worse for the customer than no number at all — it sets an expectation
the real quote then breaks. The honest sequence is: understand the space,
choose the material, then price it.

General background on why wallcovering work is normally assessed in person
before it is priced. Whether a particular company visits, and whether it
charges for that, varies.

## What can only be established on site

**Real measurements.** Rooms are rarely the dimensions people remember, walls
are rarely square, and the usable area is what remains after doors, windows
and fixtures. Material quantity is calculated from measured drops, not from
floor area.

**The condition of the surface.** The single largest unknown in any
wallcovering project. What is currently on the wall, what is under it, whether
it will come off cleanly, whether there is damage or damp. A photograph shows
none of this reliably.

**Light.** How a material reads in a space depends on where the light comes
from and how much there is. Colours and textures that look one way on a sample
in a showroom can look markedly different on a north-facing wall — which is
why samples are normally left in the actual room.

**Access and logistics.** Ceiling height, whether equipment is needed,
stairwells, how material gets into the building, what furniture has to move,
and in commercial spaces when work can happen without disrupting operations.

## Why this affects the quote

A number produced without these facts is an estimate built on assumptions,
and the assumption that usually proves wrong is the state of the wall. This is
why quotes in this trade are typically given after a visit rather than over
the phone, and why a company that quotes instantly from a description is
taking a risk that ultimately lands on the customer.

## What usually happens

A visit is normally short — measurements, a look at the surfaces, a
conversation about what the client wants and which materials suit the space
and the use. Samples may be left behind. A written quote follows.
